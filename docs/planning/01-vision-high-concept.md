# 01 - Vision / High Concept

## Pitch
Witch Hunter is a single-player, third-person open-world action RPG on a dark
Gothic continent. The world is fully 3D and rendered with modern atmospheric
tech (dynamic lighting, fog, volumetrics, weather), but every character,
creature, NPC, and interactive asset is a 2D sprite drawn in a retro 1980s/1990s
dark fantasy / pulp high fantasy illustration style. Daggerfall's scope and
structure, Dark Souls' combat feel, Morrowind's skill-by-use progression,
wrapped in a modern mood engine.

## Pitch, Updated (2026-09-11, second pass)
The game has grown a spine since this doc was first written. The refined
pitch: you are one hunter on a continent locked in a war between two
supernatural courts - a human king and queen on the side of light, an
elf-vampire king and queen on the side of dark - and the war is winnable:
the player aids one court toward total domination, or refuses both six
times, unites the underworld, and forges the Third Path. Your deeds move a
single visible moral axis; the axis decides which half of the day is yours,
which factions will speak to you, which titles you can hold, and how the
world ends. Around that spine: skill-by-use specialization (29 core skill
lines plus titled specialties), a living political map (20-30 holds that
flip between courts), an expedition survival loop (taverns, camps, warp
camp home base), and a daily food/drink loadout system. One character
build IS one playthrough, and the world changes with you.

## Reference DNA
- Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall - continent-scale map, guilds/factions, retro
  structure, fast travel, deep simulation of a lived-in world.
- Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind - skill-by-use leveling, specialization matters,
  you are bad at what you do not practice.
- Dark Souls (series) - lock-on, dodge roll, stamina discipline, deliberate
  combat pacing, camera follow, weight and consequence.
- 1980s/1990s dark fantasy illustration - Frank Frazetta, Simon Bisley, early
  Warhammer and Fighting Fantasy covers, heavy inks, grim palettes, pulp
  energy.
- Modern atmospheric render tech - fog banks, god rays, dynamic torchlight,
  volumetric night skies, weather that changes how the world reads.
- (New) Mount & Blade-style living map energy - a war between thrones that
  moves borders while you play, but experienced at street level rather than
  from a strategy layer.

## Tone
Dark, Gothic, modern-stylized retro fantasy. Not grimdark misery, but not
playful either. The world is dangerous, the factions are ambitious, and the
player is one hunter in a continent full of them. The moral axis gives the
grimness a direction: honorable hunters and dominating ones are BOTH valid
lives; the game has no narrator judging you, only consequences.

## Target Experience
- One player, one save, one character build at a time. The build IS the
  playthrough - now expressed as identity: nine stats, 29 skill lines,
  lapable specialty titles, a moral axis, and a retinue that greets you
  by name at your own camp.
- Exploration is rewarded with loot, resources, faction standing, and XP -
  and with political consequence: an accidental regicide can redraw a
  border.
- Combat encounters are designed to punish spread builds and reward focused
  ones. Souls-style: lock-on melee, manual-aim while stationary, moving
  lock-on casts, charge-up commitment.
- The retro sprite aesthetic is the identity, not a shader trick. It should
  read clearly at third-person camera distance and hold up in motion.
- The game is livable, not just beatable: taverns to save at, camps to
  defend, meals to cook for tomorrow, clans rising and falling around you.

## Non-goals (for now)
- Multiplayer or co-op of any kind.
- Live-service economy, seasonal content, or monetization hooks.
- (REVISED) Base building was a non-goal at first draft; the warp camp
  system (doc 11) has since added a purpose-built home base as a mid-game
  milestone. It is a character progression system, not a settlement sim:
  there are no villagers to govern, no economy to balance, no kingdom
  management. Keep it that way unless a future pass explicitly reopens it.
