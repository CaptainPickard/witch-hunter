I. The Hunter Illuminated

In folios of this order the hunter is drawn as the saints are drawn: bounded by gold, named in red, rendered flat against burnished ground. The Church of Mercy keeps its killers gently, and the art direction must hold both truths at once. Character presentation sits inside a devotional panel: a gilded arch, a silhouette figure, and a ledger of the soul. Every element is framed as if copied by a scribe who believes what he is writing.

II. Theme, Letter, and Palette

The Church of Mercy's art direction is an open book on a lectern: parchment, gold, red ink, and the smell of hot wax. It is the light court at its most deliberate - holy, warm, ordered - yet never soft. Gothic weight comes from tall pointed frames, dense rules, and the permanent shadow at the bottom of every panel, where the candle does not reach. Mercy is the subject, not the mood.

Mood Statement

The world is a page that has been wept upon, prayed over, and corrected in red. The hunter walks through a manuscript where the illuminator is still working: gold leaf is laid over rot, rubrics mark the graves. Warmth is active, earned light - candle, dawn, hearth, vigil-flame - never ambient. Where the light stops, the Veil begins.

Typography Direction

RUBRICATED SMALL CAPS
Headings and labels: red-ink small capitals, wide letterspacing, as if written between the lines by the rubricator's hand. Two weights only - bold for plate names, regular for marginalia.
Ledger Body
Body text is a warm serif with tall x-height, set justified with hyphenation, on a generous 1.6 line. Paragraph openers take a three-line drop cap in red over a gold ground.
Marginalia - italic, smaller, brown ink, always to the outer edge, always a whisper: a warning, a memory, a debt.
System Stacks (no external fonts)
Georgia / Times New Roman - body, ledger entries.
Palatino / Book Antiqua - headings, name plates, small caps via letterspacing.
Courier New - only for item counts, weights, and numeric columns, as a ledger hand.
Numbers are always followed by a spelled unit in italic: 7 vigor, 14 days, 24 hours. The manuscript never shows a bare integer.

Texture and Lighting Language

Texture: fiber grain in every surface; a faint laid-paper ribbing; foxed spots at panel corners. Sprites carry a hand-inked outline the weight of a quill stroke, and gold is reserved for what the Church blesses - never a texture, always an application, laid in patches that catch light at a rake.

Lighting: one candle logic everywhere. A single warm source, deep falloff, gold bounce on parchment surfaces. Weather in this palette: THE RED VIGIL reads as gilded storm-light on every gold surface at once; THE PALE TIDE cools the parchment to bone and dims the gold to pewter. Rubric red survives both.

III. Settings - Grove and Hearth

Two settings are illuminated side by side, as a book pairs a psalm with its gloss: the Darkwood, where the Church of Mercy sends its hunters to die politely, and the parish tavern, where the hunter is remembered by name. One is painted in ink and shadow; the other in gold and candlelight. Together they state the game's whole emotional range.

Biome - The Darkwood

The Darkwood is drawn as an unfinished illumination: the ink runs and the gold has not been laid yet. Fog-choked old growth rendered in iron-gall greens and blacks, with a single failing sun-disc low on the horizon - the light court's motif, barely surviving. Coven marks appear as red rubric glyphs scratched into bark, wrong-handed, like a parody of scripture. Sprite direction: tall vertical trunks in 2-3 depth layers, hunter sprite small against them, torch-glow the only warm value in frame.

Settlement - The Gilded Fawn, a Tavern of the Parish

The tavern is the manuscript's gold-leaf page: the one place the illumination is finished. Warm amber windows, a gilded stag sign over the road, candle-glow spilling on cobbles. The save-point is an illuminated initial in the corner of the scene - a candle-lit board by the hearth where the hunter's name is written into the tavern's guestbook, in red, the moment you rent a room. Departure is dramatized as the scribe's act of closing the book.

IV. Situations - Four Marginal Vignettes

In the manuscript's margins the scribe drew the small truths: the inn, the wolf, the war, the grave. Each vignette below is a marginal illustration with its own rubric, showing how UI and scene mood behave in four signature moments.

(a) Vespers at the Hearth

The save moment is a small rite: the guestbook board by the hearth glows amber, the hunter's silhouette kneels one knee. UI text is rubric red on parchment: Room taken. Your resurrection is bound to this hearth. No meter, no spinner - the candle guttering is the progress.

(b) The Pale Tide Ambush

Wolves of the wild strain close under a moon-fog; the hunter's censer is the single warm point in a silver frame. UI turns manuscript-dangerous: health is a red rubric line being un-written, edge to center, with a margin note - Faith holds. Breath. Loose at the second howl.

(c) Council Before the Wall

Officers around a trestle under a Storm-Call-darkened sky; the keep's sun-banner burns low on the horizon. Dialogue UI is a council page: speaker names in gold small caps, options as ruled choices in red - Bless the wall. Burn the causeway. Send for the Mercy tents. The chosen line is illuminated; the rest fades to brown ink.

(d) The Fourteen Days

A mourner's tent in the camp community; fourteen small red marks line the panel border, one struck through per in-game day. The dead are remembered as marginalia: each name written into the camp's roll in brown ink, then re-inked in red on the final day. UI never counts the dead; it keeps the list.

V. Notes of the Scribe

Marginal observations, doubts, and open questions, set down in the rubricator's hand for the illuminators who follow.

  • Open question: how far can gold leaf go before it reads as reward-spam? Reserve a hard budget - gold appears only on blessed things and save points, and audit every screen for compliance.
  • Open question: does the rubric red survive as readable on the darkest Darkwood night frames, or does marginalia need a bone-paper chip behind it in low-light scenes?
  • Open question: drop caps on every paragraph may be too liturgical for mid-combat UI. Propose drop caps only for book-like screens (ledger, map legend, quest folios) and plain rubric headers for the HUD.
  • Gap: no decision yet on how THE PALE TIDE's silver wash should treat the gold leaf - pewter shift is proposed but untested against sprite readability at 512px.
  • Gap: the stat names (Vigor, Resolve, Faith, Sight, Steel, Grace, Lore, Craft, Silence) are invented for this folio; confirm against the shared nine-stat design before they are treated as canon.
  • Gap: equipment strip shows 8 slots but weight rules live only in prose; the ledger needs a defined carry-weight presentation (margin note vs. column figure).
  • Question for lighting: candle-amber is the only warm source in UI, but THE RED VIGIL floods the world with storm-gold. Where is the line between weather light and UI light?
  • Concern: the mourning vignette's fourteen struck marks need a legible in-world counterpart - confirm the camp's mourning roll is a readable object, not only a UI list.
  • Note: sprite frames (4-8) are sparse for the censer burn animation; flag for the animation pass whether a dedicated 8-frame loop is budgeted.
Here ends the third folio of the Witch Hunter art direction, written for the Church of Mercy, in gold, in red, and in iron-gall ink.
