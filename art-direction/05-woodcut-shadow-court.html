II. Theme - Mood, Type, Ink and Light

Block D - Mood Statement

The World Is A Printed Rumor

In the underworld nothing is written down, so everything is printed by hand. Wanted posters, coven woodcuts, toll manifests, altar rubbings - the truth arrives coarse, second-hand, and slightly wrong every time it is recarved. The player reads the war of two courts the way a commoner does: through broadsides nailed to tavern doors and seals broken on smuggler manifests.

Every image in this register should feel like it was pulled by hand in a back room: ink that skips, edges that bite crooked, paper that shows through. Never glossy, never precise, never machine-made.

Block E - Typography Direction

WOODBLOCK DISPLAY

All-caps display set like cut poster letters: heavy, slab, slightly uneven, tight vertical rhythm. Letterforms may lean one degree off true - the block was not planed level. Use for names, warnings, and section seals only.

Manifest Hand

Monospace courier for ledgers, stats, prices, tallies - the clerk's voice of the underworld. Values read as stamps, not text.

Serif Body

A workman serif (Georgia class) for narration - the voice of a person recounting, not a system informing.

Block F - Texture and Lighting Language
  • Every dark shape is cut ink: hard silhouette edges, no gradients, no soft shadows. Depth comes from hatch density, not blur.
  • Light is described by omission - a torch is a ragged white hole bitten out of the black; moonlight is thin parallel cuts of silver-gray.
  • Ink skip: at 10-15% of any filled shape, paper shows through as speckle. Applied via texture pass, never hand-placed.
  • Weather reads as print weather: Red Vigil is an oxblood wash across the whole plate; Pale Tide is a teal-gray fog band of horizontal hatching.
  • Damage and dread are printed crooked: when the Veil presses close, the frame itself misregisters one degree.

III. Setting - Darkwood Panel and Tavern Panel

Biome Panel - Darkwood

The Owl-Road

The underworld's highway through the old growth. Rendered as a near-solid ink print: two colossal trunks cut as pure black, the canopy a single ragged silhouette, fog as horizontal teal hatching punched between them. Witch-charms hang at eye height - the covens mark their toll. Depth is flat by design: foreground, fog-band, treeline. Three planes only, like a print pulled in three passes. An eye of teal in the ground shadow tells the player something has already seen them.

Settlement Panel - Tavern Save-Point

The Broken Keel, River Landing

A neutral save-anchor on a smuggler route. The tavern reads as a single black blockprint mass against a night plate, its lit windows bitten out of the ink as ragged paper holes - the visual promise of rest. A teal door-glow marks neutral ground; an oxblood nailed sign (the broken-keel charge) marks whose route it stands on. The wanted poster beside the door is a recurring world-detail: sometimes it is the player's own face. Respawn here prints a small stamped seal over the minimap, not a bright marker.

IV. Situations - Four Vignettes

Vignette A - Tavern Save-Point

Save UI as a stamped inn ledger: a coarse ink bar across the plate with carved option-stamps. Resting prints a teal seal on the page corner that stays until the next death.

Vignette B - Night Ambush

Enemies are black-on-black shapes that resolve only when the lantern cone bites them into paper. Wolves show twin teal eye-cuts before their bodies resolve. Health bar is a hatched oxblood tally, drawn by hand.

Vignette C - Siege War Council

Council scenes are printed as manifest pages: a map-table block with court charges (sun-gold name plate vs teal), an oxblood assault arrow, and a broken-sun seal above. War decisions stamp directly onto the plan.

Vignette D - Camp Mourning (14-Day Remembrance)

Mourning prints as a tally page: fourteen cut marks, the fallen rendered as a faceless ink plank with an oxblood ring. Each dawn one mark is crossed in teal. No music, no UI glow - only the count.

V. Notes - Open Questions and Gaps

  • Sprite scale question: the woodcut register wants hard silhouette edges, but 512-1024px sprites at 4-8 frames may wobble the "cut" line in motion. Do we accept boil (natural, hand-printed feel) or lock every frame to a shared silhouette pass?
  • Teal vs oxblood priority is undefined at the UI level. Proposal: teal owns menus and neutral-ground chrome, oxblood owns damage, debt, and death states - but confirm no oxblood bleed into menus.
  • Ink-skip speckle needs a spec: is it a shader pass over all sprites, an art-baked texture per sheet, or both? Performance cost on true-3D world with full sprite population untested.
  • The "misregistered frame" Veil-dread effect is described but unmeasured: one degree off what - the sprite frame, the screen vignette, or the UI frame only? Needs a playable feel test.
  • Wanted-poster system: if posters can show the player's face after notoriety, we need a carve-style generator for player portraits (silhouette + 2 accent marks) rather than a rendered image.
  • Weather plates (Red Vigil, Pale Tide, Storm-Calls) sketched here as washes and hatch bands; need a decision on whether they are post-process passes over the 3D scene or per-biome sprite repaints.
  • Readability risk: black-on-black enemy design (Vignette B) depends entirely on lantern light logic. If light rendering changes, this scene language breaks. Flag as a dependency, not a style choice.
  • Stat tallies are stamped in the manifest table here; other art-direction pages likely use bars or orbs. Confirm which page owns the canonical UI stat language and whether the woodcut plate is a skin over it or a separate underworld-only UI mode.
  • No decision yet on how the three-altar sites read in this register versus the Light/Dark court registers - the Deep Altar is underworld-kept, so it should get the teal seal treatment, but it is not drawn here.
  • ASCII-only constraint kept for this plate; confirm the woodcut display face will be a custom in-game bitmap font so no unicode decorative characters are ever needed at runtime.
