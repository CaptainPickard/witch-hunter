II.

Theme

Mood, Type, Palette

The mood statement: a hunter stands between two courts that would own the sky, and the only honest weather is the storm overhead. Deliberate, gothic, painted - never miserable, never playful.

Typography Direction

Bold Condensed Display
Cover type is the masthead voice: heavy condensed caps for titles and taglines, warm parchment serif italics for flavor copy, narrow sans small print for data. Titles get a hard drop shadow and a rust-colored rim. Taglines read like paperback back covers: short, imperative, fatalistic.
Title / tagline: condensed grotesque, all caps, tracked tight. - Body flavor: serif italic, parchment tone. - UI labels: narrow sans caps, letterspaced 0.25em. - Numerals: tabular, inked.

III.

Setting

One Wild Biome, One Hearth

Two panels that bookend every journey: the Darkwood you must walk through, and the tavern hearth that is the only place the world lets you stop.

The Darkwood - Fog-Choked Old Growth

Composition rule: trunks as vertical ink bars, one rust focal glyph, fog as horizontal paint. Covens mark their ground in broken-sun sigils.

The Guttering Lantern - Tavern Save-Point

Rent a room. Rest. Set your respawn. The lantern is the save light - warm rust against the ink, faction-neutral ground inside.
IV.

Situations

Four Cover Panels, Four Moods

Each vignette is framed like a pulp cover inset: silhouette, rim light, one accent focal point, a short imperative caption. Scene mood carries the UI truth - the clock, the weather, the remembrance.

a. The Save - Last Room at the Lantern

REST IS A DECISION
Room taken, respawn set. The ledger signs in quill ink. Clock reads half the night already gone; departure time is chosen here, at the fire.

b. Night Ambush - Wolves in the Pale Fog

THE PALE TIDE WAKES THEM
Moon-fog on the moors lifts the blight's drain and wakes the wild strains. Eyes first, shapes second - enemies reveal as rim light before body ink. Night travel is a survival choice.

c. Siege War Council - Storm-Call Over the Hold

THEY PIN THE SKY FIRST
Storm-calls nail a Veil-charged front over the enemy hold before the siege. Banners of both courts over one table; the map shows the route the weather will decide.

d. Camp Mourning - The Fourteenth Day Remembrance

FOURTEEN DAYS, THEN THE ROAD
The camp remembers its dead for fourteen in-game days: marker stones, lowered colors, a talisman hung per name. Fire burns low and rust, never cheerful. Mourning is honored, not dwelt in.
V.

Notes

Open Questions
  • Sprite cast vs. pulp page: confirm whether the masthead hero should also exist as a game sprite pose, or whether cover compositions stay promo-only.
  • Accent discipline: Veil Rust (#C14A24) is reserved for danger, blight, and focal elements - confirm the Light Court's sun-gold does not visually collide with it in shared scenes.
  • Condensed display faces vary across systems (Haettenschweiler vs Impact); a shipped title logo should be baked as SVG paths rather than trusted to font stacks.
  • Weather readability without a UI meter: does the Pale Tide fog need its own palette ramp distinct from Darkwood fog, or is a moon disc cue enough?
  • The 14-day mourning period - should the remembrance banner visibly gain a talisman per fallen name, and does the count render on the banner itself?
  • Tavern save-point: should the respawn glyph on the door be diegetic (carved sigil) or a subtle UI overlay on approach?
  • Portrait frame style: oval oil-portrait frame reads paperback; alternative is a plain inked rectangle closer to in-game sprite presentation. Needs one choice.
  • Blotted line weight for sprites (heavy ink) is close to the pulp cover linework, but rim-light pass may cost per-frame - verify budget for 4-8 frame animations.
