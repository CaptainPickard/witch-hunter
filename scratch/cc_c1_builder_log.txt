B-TRI is a fourth sampler I added. It reads the same cached height grid as B, but follows the same two triangles per cell that the mesh draws, so it matches the rendered surface exactly.

**Verdict:** CC-C4's `heightAt` should be B-TRI. Fallback order: B bilinear, then A analytic, then C raycast (debug only). The brief's expectation that A or B would come in under 0.05 at S=140 did not hold. A misses because its finest noise layer has a 5.6-unit wavelength, which the 4-unit mesh cells can't represent. B misses because bilinear blending doesn't follow the mesh's triangles.

**climbFactor and slopes**
- Slope in degrees (p50 / p90 / p99 / max):
  - analytic: 4.7 / 9.0 / 12.9 / 19.5
  - mesh faces at S=140: 4.5 / 8.6 / 12.5 / 18.2
- The demo ships climbFactor 0.2: running is blocked above 11.3° (2.2% of faces), walking above 16.7° (0.02%).
- I picked 0.2 only so you can actually see blocking. The hills top out around 18°, so a sensible value like 0.6 would never block anything. You can change it live with `[` and `]`.

**Recommendation:**
- **Mesh density:** use S=140: 39,200 triangles, about 2% of the 2M budget, one draw call. S=180 adds 65% more triangles for little visible gain with flat shading.
- **Sampler:** B-TRI is the single source of truth. Player, enemies, props, corpses and the camp ghost all read the grid that built the mesh, with zero error at about 0.08 µs per call.
- **climbFactor:** ship 0.6 (31° running / 42° walking) as a safety net for rocks, roots and camp edges. Better: switch to a slope-angle cap that doesn't depend on speed. The brief's formula lets a walking actor climb steeper slopes than a running one.
- **Warnings:**
  - A raycast costs 1.3–3.2 ms per call because three.js has no acceleration structure for it here. Keep it out of per-frame gameplay.
  - Building the mesh is synchronous (27 ms at S=140). Build once at region load and never on the fly.
  - Fog hides the plane's edge from spawn, but not once you're within about 60 units of the edge. CC-C3 needs a margin or a skirt beyond the walkable area.
  - Any future level-of-detail or re-triangulation of the mesh breaks B-TRI's exactness unless the grid is rebuilt from the final mesh.
